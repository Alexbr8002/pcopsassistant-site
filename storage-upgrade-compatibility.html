Storage guide

Storage upgrades start with compatibility

Last updated November 9, 2026

Capacity and benchmark speed do not tell you whether a drive will work in your PC.

Identify the interface

PCs may use 2.5-inch SATA, M.2 SATA or M.2 NVMe storage. M.2 is a form factor, not a guarantee of protocol compatibility.

Confirm physical size

M.2 modules can differ in length, while 2.5-inch drives need the correct bay and cable. Check the computer documentation before purchase.

Check slot availability

An installed drive may already occupy the only compatible slot, so decide whether the upgrade adds storage or replaces the boot drive.

Compare performance for your workload

PCIe generation and peak sequential speed do not describe the whole upgrade. Compare independent application, low-queue-depth and sustained-write tests for the exact capacity and firmware. A newer interface or a DRAM cache alone does not establish better everyday performance.

Check power, cooling and total cost

Confirm single- or double-sided clearance, heatsink space and the computer maker's requirements. Include the drive, any required cooling or adapters, migration and return terms in the comparison. A faster drive cannot exceed the host interface's limits; compare the benefit at the speed your PC supports.

Verify the offer before buying

Check the exact model and capacity, seller, condition, warranty, current price and availability. An out-of-stock listing or an old review price is not a current purchase offer.

Plan data migration separately

Cloning or reinstalling Windows is a different decision from hardware compatibility. Back up important data before changing a boot device.
